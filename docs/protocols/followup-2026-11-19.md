# Follow-up cohort of 19 November 2026: retesting the three missed targets

Recorded before the first run of `.github/workflows/followup.yml`. Hashes of the
instruments are in [followup-2026-11-19.json](followup-2026-11-19.json). The gate is the
frozen `browser-candidate-2026-10-30`, measured with the collector at its frozen
`SOURCE_SHA` (30ca833); nothing in the gate changes.

The [18 November results](../extensions-2026-11-18.md) missed three targets: rare-stall
detection by the tail signal (67/100), interleaved A/A under load (1/100, Wilson upper
bound 5.4%) and TBT +10% under load (61/100). Those results stand as reported. This
cohort tests changes made after looking at them; a pass here is a new result about the
changed method, not a repair of the old one.

## Sample

Each run collects 30 jobs, all on `ubuntu-24.04` hosted runners. The workflow
dispatches the next run until **25 complete runs** (all 30 jobs stored) exist under
`prospective/followup-2026-11-19/` on the data branch. The analysis uses the **first N
valid jobs per type in acquisition order**; later jobs are reported as extra data.
Validity rules are those of 18 November; for `pin-*` jobs the recorded affinity of the
load generator must also be vCPUs 2,3. Wilson 95% intervals; resampling of acquisition
days is reported beside them.

| Job type | Per run | N | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tail-rare` | 6 | 150 | frozen pilot rare-stall collector, 3 of 20 PR loads with an 800 ms stall |
| `tail-aa` | 4 | 100 | the same page without stalls |
| `load-aa` | 8 | 200 | A/A, heavy page, interleaved, background CPU load as on 18 November |
| `load-lt10-r60` | 4 | 100 | long-task 99 ms (TBT ≈ +10%) under the same load, **60 loads** per variant |
| `pin-aa` | 4 | 100 | A/A under the same load, load generator on vCPUs 2,3, browser on 0,1 |
| `pin-lt10` | 4 | 100 | long-task 99 ms, as `pin-aa`, 20 loads |

The background load is `scripts/background-load.mjs` with the settings of 18 November
(two busy threads, on/off periods 5–60 s, seeded per job). Seeds: rare-stall jobs use
the two predeclared pilot seeds, alternating by run number and replicate.

## B2. Count rule for rare long tasks

**Why the old rule missed.** With three slow loads of twenty and quiet base loads,
Welch's t stays near 1.8 whatever the stall length, so p hovers around 0.04 and noise
pushes it above 0.05. In addition, on the fastest runners a stall sometimes ends after
Lighthouse stops measuring: per stalled load, TBT rose by about 740 ms or by almost
nothing, with little in between.

**Rule** (`scripts/tail_exceedance.py`): per page, threshold = median(base TBT) +
max(100 ms, 20% of that median); the page warns when at least two PR loads are above
the threshold and no base load is. Advisory, as before: the verdict is unchanged.

**How it was chosen (exploratory).** On data already collected
(`scripts/calibrate-tail-exceedance.py`, all variants looked at in
`results/2026-11-19/tail_exceedance_calibration.csv`): 90/100 `tail-rare` jobs warned,
0 of 200 confirmatory four-page A/A suites and 0 of 300 A/A jobs (`tail-aa`,
`cls-aa-reserved`, `load-aa-abab`). The chosen constants sit on a plateau: thresholds
from 50 to 100 ms and from 0 to 25% give 88–90 detections and at most one false
warning when two slow PR loads and no slow base load are required. Requiring one slow
load gives 96–98 detections but up to 18 false suites; counting base loads as an offset
(PR ≥ base + 2) gives 21–28 false warnings under load. These numbers are calibration,
not evidence.

**Targets.** `tail-rare`: Wilson lower bound ≥ 80%. False warnings, for each of
`tail-aa`, `load-aa`, `pin-aa`: Wilson upper bound ≤ 5%. The old Welch rule and the
gate's own TBT decision on `tail-rare` are reported beside it.

## C1. Interleaved A/A under load, larger sample

Same load and page as `load-aa-abab` on 18 November, N = 200 fixed in advance.
**Target:** gate alarm on any metric, Wilson upper bound ≤ 5%. Alarms on the timing
metrics alone are reported separately; the target counts all four metrics.

## C3. TBT +10% under load

Two independent changes, each with its own target, Wilson lower bound ≥ 80% for TBT
detection:

- **More loads** (`load-lt10-r60`): 60 loads per variant, interleaved. The frozen policy
  is defined for 10 or 20 loads, so these jobs are evaluated with the same per-page
  statistics (one-sided Mann–Whitney, Holm over the four metrics, frozen margins,
  validity rules with 60 expected loads). This is a research profile: it shows what the
  sensitivity costs in machine time, not a policy change. There is no 60-load A/A job;
  its false-alarm rate is not measured here.
- **Separate vCPUs** (`pin-lt10`, `pin-aa`): the load generator runs under
  `taskset -c 2,3`, the collector and its Chrome under `taskset -c 0,1`, 20 loads, the
  frozen gate. This stands for a runner limited to its own CPUs (for example
  `cpuset_cpus` of the GitLab Docker executor). The hosted runner's vCPUs may share
  physical cores; the topology (`lscpu -e`) is stored with each job and reported.
  `pin-aa` target: gate alarm Wilson upper bound ≤ 5%.

Within-job noise (MAD/median of the base loads) is reported per job type.

## Not changed

Policy, margins, correction, exclusions, the collector and the load generator. Any
failure of a target is reported as a failure. If a run fails, it is dispatched again by
hand; its stored valid jobs count in acquisition order. Analysis:
`scripts/evaluate-followup.py`, hash recorded with this protocol.
