## Complete candidate gate

The URL/recorded-data comparison uses four metrics per page (FCP/LCP/TBT/CLS),
one-sided MWU, Holm over the whole check and documented practical margins.
This differs from historical RQ3, which used two-sided tests and included Speed
Index rather than CLS, with different TBT margin handling.

Retrospective evaluation at 10 and 20 loads gives **one alarm in 133 complete
four-page A/A suites (0.75%; Wilson 95% interval 0.13–4.14%)**. Seventeen historical
suites are invalid for this comparison (missing pages or insufficient samples),
not successful quiet checks. These are historical observations, not a prospective
error-rate guarantee or a reason to tune thresholds against this corpus again.

At twenty loads, whole-page detection is 110/110 for nominal +2% script-delay,
110/110 for nominal +10% long-task, and 104/110 for nominal +5% layout-shift.
These are **any-metric** detections, not necessarily detection on the injected
target metric. Nominal and measured sizes differ. The lower nominal TBT cases
remain difficult, and the +2% CLS case is normally below the absolute margin.
Do not combine these numbers with historical per-metric detection rates.

The policy is a candidate, with source hashes and a prospective acquisition
boundary recorded under docs/protocols. Full completion criteria include a later,
untouched CI set, resource-budget comparisons and external-validity checks.

## Distribution shape and a new experimental method

A permutation test of the mean difference was added outside the historical set
of eleven rules. It assumes exchangeability under its null; it is not a solution
to arbitrary heteroscedasticity or serial dependence. Reproducible simulations
compare twelve rules on common samples: eight scenarios, 500 experiments each,
at ten and twenty loads per variant.

For a 15% probability of an additional 100-unit stall, n=20:

| Rule | Detection |
|---|---:|
| MWU plus observed median effect >1% | 13.2% |
| Brunner–Munzel | 16.2% |
| Welch t | 54.2% |
| Permutation mean | 45.8% |

The mean rises while the median changes little. The implication is a limitation
of the chosen endpoint, not a universal ranking of the tests. In an AR(1) null,
sequential assignment also inflates false alarms for several methods; alternating
assignment changes that result. The full simulated tables include uncertainty and
all scenarios. They must not be described as measured browser results.

## Implementation and what remains

Implemented locally: whole-series quality checks, URL/replay comparison, temporary
Git-ref build orchestration, JSON/Markdown reports, timing metadata, a composite
GitHub Action, and research scripts. Local automated tests and browser runs are
reported separately. No publication or hosted execution of the new Action is
implied by its presence in the repository.

Required remaining research: prospective validation, measured rare-stall browser
experiments, equal-time evaluation, repeated sitespeed/local sessions, and robust
history calibration. The history prototype still produces too many alarms on
some unchanged series; it must remain advisory. See research-completion.md.

Native application support is a future adapter/research programme, not included
in the browser completion claim. The statistical sample kernel has been separated
from Lighthouse data loading to avoid unnecessarily coupling future collectors.
